## What is MEV?

**Maximal Extractable Value (MEV)** refers to profit that can be extracted by reordering, inserting, or censoring transactions within a block. In practice, it often means sophisticated actors profiting at the expense of regular traders.

On Ethereum, MEV extraction totals millions of dollars daily. Understanding and protecting against it is essential for active DEX users.

## Common MEV Attacks

### Sandwich Attacks

The most common attack on DEX traders.

**How it works:**
1. You submit a swap: Buy 10 ETH for USDC
2. Attacker sees your pending transaction in the mempool
3. Attacker front-runs: Buys ETH before you, raising the price
4. Your transaction executes at the now-higher price
5. Attacker back-runs: Sells ETH after you, profiting from price impact

**Your loss:** You receive fewer tokens than a fair market price would provide.

### Front-Running

Attacker sees a profitable transaction and copies it, executing first.

**Example:** You spot an arbitrage opportunity and submit a transaction. A bot copies your strategy and submits with higher gas, capturing the opportunity instead.

### Back-Running

Less harmful than sandwiches. Attacker executes immediately after your transaction to capture residual arbitrage.

**Example:** Your large buy creates a price discrepancy. Arbitrageur trades to restore balance. This does not directly harm you but captures value your trade created.

### Liquidation Hunting

Bots monitor DeFi positions approaching liquidation thresholds and compete to execute liquidations for the bonus.

## Quantifying MEV Impact

Average MEV extracted per trade varies by:
- Trade size: Larger trades are more profitable to attack
- Token liquidity: Illiquid tokens have more price impact
- Slippage tolerance: Higher tolerance means more room for extraction
- Time of day: Congested periods have more competition

Studies estimate sandwich attacks extract 0.1-0.5% of large trade values on average. On a $10,000 trade, this is $10-50 in lost value.

## Protection Strategies

### Use Private Transaction Pools

Instead of broadcasting to the public mempool, send transactions to private pools:

**Flashbots Protect:** Free RPC that routes through Flashbots builders
- Setup: Add custom RPC to wallet
- URL: rpc.flashbots.net

**MEV Blocker:** Coalition of builders offering frontrunning protection
- Similar RPC setup
- Rebates a portion of MEV back to users

**How it helps:** Transactions are not visible to searchers until included in a block.

### Limit Slippage Tolerance

Tight slippage limits reduce sandwich profitability:
- 0.5% slippage: Sandwich must work within this margin
- If attack would cause >0.5% impact, transaction reverts
- Attacker wastes gas on failed attempt

**Tradeoff:** Transactions fail more often in volatile markets.

### Use DEX Aggregators with Protection

Some aggregators route through protected channels:

**CowSwap:** Batch auctions match trades peer-to-peer when possible, avoiding on-chain exposure entirely.

**1inch Fusion:** Resolvers compete to fill orders, often providing better execution than public pools.

### Time Your Trades

MEV is most aggressive during:
- High volatility periods
- Major news events
- Congested network conditions

Trading during quieter periods reduces competition from MEV bots.

### Split Large Trades

Instead of one $100,000 swap:
- Execute 10 x $10,000 swaps over time
- Each individual trade has less price impact
- Harder for sandwichers to profit from smaller amounts

**Tradeoff:** More gas costs, longer execution.

## Chain-Specific Considerations

### Ethereum Mainnet

Highest MEV extraction. Essential to use protection:
- Flashbots Protect for transactions
- CowSwap for swaps
- Tight slippage settings

### Solana

Different architecture reduces some MEV but does not eliminate it:
- Transactions processed in parallel
- Less mempool visibility
- Still vulnerable to collocated searchers
- Jito protocol manages much of Solana MEV

### Layer 2s (Arbitrum, Optimism)

Currently less MEV than mainnet but growing:
- Centralized sequencers have MEV power
- Some L2s implement fair ordering
- Protection tools less mature

### BSC and Other Chains

Varies by chain design:
- Check if mempool is public
- Investigate local MEV landscape
- Use chain-specific protection when available

## Evaluating MEV Protection Tools

### Flashbots Protect

**Pros:** Free, simple setup, reliable
**Cons:** Transactions may be slightly slower

### MEV Blocker

**Pros:** Rebates portion of captured MEV, multiple builder support
**Cons:** Newer, smaller builder set

### CowSwap

**Pros:** Strong MEV protection, gasless trades
**Cons:** Only for Ethereum, requires specific token pairs

### Private RPCs

**Pros:** Direct protection from mempool exposure
**Cons:** Must trust the RPC operator, potential centralization

## Setting Up Protection

### MetaMask + Flashbots Protect

1. Open MetaMask Settings
2. Networks > Add Network
3. Enter Flashbots RPC details:
   - Network Name: Flashbots Protect
   - RPC URL: https://rpc.flashbots.net
   - Chain ID: 1
   - Symbol: ETH
4. Switch to this network for protected transactions

### Using CowSwap

1. Visit swap.cow.fi
2. Connect wallet
3. Trades automatically use batch auctions
4. No additional setup required
