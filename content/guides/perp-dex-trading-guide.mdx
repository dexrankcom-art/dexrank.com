## What Are Perpetual Futures?

Perpetual futures (perps) are derivative contracts that let you speculate on asset prices with leverage, without expiration dates. Unlike traditional futures that settle on a specific date, perps can be held indefinitely.

On decentralized perp DEXs like GMX, dYdX, and Hyperliquid, you trade these contracts from self-custody with no KYC.

## How Perps Work

### Long and Short Positions

**Long:** You profit when price goes up. A 10x long on ETH at $2,000 gives you exposure to $20,000 of ETH with $2,000 collateral.

**Short:** You profit when price goes down. A 10x short on ETH at $2,000 profits if ETH falls.

### Leverage Mechanics

Leverage multiplies both gains and losses.

**Example:** 10x leverage, $1,000 collateral
- If ETH moves +5%: You gain $500 (50% profit on collateral)
- If ETH moves -5%: You lose $500 (50% loss on collateral)
- If ETH moves -10%: Position liquidated (100% loss)

Higher leverage means smaller price moves can wipe out your position.

### Margin and Collateral

Your deposited funds serve as collateral. Margin requirements vary by platform:

- **Initial margin:** Amount needed to open position
- **Maintenance margin:** Minimum to keep position open
- **Cross margin:** All collateral backs all positions
- **Isolated margin:** Specific collateral for specific positions

## Funding Rates

Funding rates keep perp prices aligned with spot prices.

### How Funding Works

Every 8 hours (on most platforms):
- If perp price > spot price: Longs pay shorts
- If perp price < spot price: Shorts pay longs

### Funding Rate Impact

**Positive funding:** Bullish market, expensive to hold longs
**Negative funding:** Bearish market, expensive to hold shorts

Annualized funding can reach 50-100% during extreme market conditions. This significantly impacts holding costs for longer-term positions.

### Funding Rate Strategy

- Check funding before entering positions
- Avoid opening in the direction of extreme funding
- Consider funding as part of total position cost

## Liquidation

If your position loses enough that margin falls below maintenance requirements, the protocol liquidates it.

### Liquidation Process

1. Price moves against your position
2. Unrealized loss approaches your collateral
3. Liquidation engine closes your position
4. You lose most or all of your collateral
5. Some platforms charge liquidation penalties

### Liquidation Price Calculation

For a 10x long opened at $2,000:
- Your $1,000 collateral supports $10,000 exposure
- 10% price drop ($200) wipes out collateral
- Liquidation price approximately $1,800 (varies by platform)

### Avoiding Liquidation

**Use lower leverage:** 2-5x is much safer than 10-20x

**Set stop losses:** Exit before liquidation

**Monitor positions:** Markets move 24/7

**Add collateral:** If price moves against you, add margin

## Major Perp DEXs

### GMX (Arbitrum, Avalanche)

Uses GLP liquidity pool model. Traders trade against the pool.

**Characteristics:**
- Zero price impact for trades under caps
- GLP earns from trader losses
- 30+ trading pairs
- $100M+ TVL

### dYdX

Order book model with off-chain matching, on-chain settlement.

**Characteristics:**
- High throughput (1000+ TPS)
- Own L1 chain (Cosmos-based)
- Closest to CEX experience
- Deep liquidity on major pairs

### Hyperliquid

Newer entrant with custom L1 and aggressive fee structure.

**Characteristics:**
- Sub-cent trading fees
- Fast execution
- Growing liquidity
- Airdrop history attracts volume

## Risk Management

### Position Sizing

Never risk more than 1-5% of trading capital on a single trade. With leverage, this means small position sizes relative to your account.

**Example:** $10,000 account, 2% risk, 10x leverage
- Maximum loss: $200
- Position size: $2,000 exposure
- Collateral: $200

### Stop Losses

Set stop losses when entering positions. Determine your exit point before the trade, not after.

**Types:**
- Hard stop: Automatic close at price
- Mental stop: Manual close (requires discipline)
- Trailing stop: Follows price to lock in gains

### Correlation Risk

Multiple positions in correlated assets (BTC and ETH) compound risk. If crypto drops, both positions lose simultaneously.

Diversify across uncorrelated positions or limit total directional exposure.

## Trading Strategies

### Trend Following

Trade in direction of established trend. Use longer timeframes to identify trend, shorter for entry.

**Entry:** Price breaks resistance in uptrend
**Exit:** Trailing stop or trend reversal signal

### Mean Reversion

Trade against extreme moves, expecting return to average.

**Entry:** Price deviates significantly from moving average
**Exit:** Price returns to mean or stop loss hits

### Funding Rate Arbitrage

Capture funding by taking the paying side while hedging spot exposure.

**Example:** Positive funding on perps
1. Long spot ETH
2. Short perp ETH
3. Collect funding from shorts
4. Delta neutral if positions equal

Requires capital efficiency calculation and monitoring.

## Costs and Considerations

### Fee Structure

Typical perp DEX fees:
- Opening/closing: 0.05-0.10%
- Funding: Variable (8-hour intervals)
- Liquidation penalty: 1-5%

### Slippage

Large positions move prices, especially on smaller platforms. Check depth before sizing trades.

### Oracle Risk

Perp prices depend on oracle feeds. Oracle manipulation or delays can cause unexpected liquidations.

## Getting Started Safely

1. **Start with paper trading:** dYdX testnet, practice without risk
2. **Use minimal leverage:** 2-3x maximum while learning
3. **Trade small:** Risk only what you can lose entirely
4. **Focus on one pair:** Learn ETH-USD or BTC-USD deeply
5. **Keep a trading journal:** Track every trade and its rationale

## Summary

Perpetual DEXs offer leveraged exposure to crypto assets without centralized exchange counterparty risk. The tradeoffs include liquidation risk, funding costs, and smart contract exposure.

Approach perp trading with caution. Leverage amplifies both wins and losses. Most leverage traders lose money over time. Proper risk management is essential for survival.
