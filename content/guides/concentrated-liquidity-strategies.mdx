## Concentrated Liquidity Fundamentals

Uniswap v3 introduced concentrated liquidity: instead of providing liquidity across all prices, LPs choose a specific price range. Capital efficiency increases dramatically within the range, but positions earn nothing outside it.

A position concentrated in a 10% range can achieve up to 4000x more capital efficiency than v2's full-range approach.

## Range Selection Strategies

### Symmetric Ranges

Center your range around current price with equal upside and downside.

**Example:** ETH at $2,000
- Range: $1,800 to $2,200 (10% each direction)
- Earns fees if ETH stays between these prices
- Position goes 100% ETH below $1,800, 100% USDC above $2,200

**Best for:** Markets without strong directional bias.

### Asymmetric Ranges

Skew your range based on expected price movement.

**Bullish example:** ETH at $2,000
- Range: $1,900 to $2,500
- More upside capture, less downside protection
- Position accumulates ETH on pullbacks

**Best for:** When you have directional conviction.

### Full-Range Positions

Setting min to 0 and max to infinity mimics Uniswap v2.

**When useful:**
- Extremely volatile pairs where any range risks going out of range
- Tokens with unpredictable price discovery
- Passive LPs who cannot actively manage

**Tradeoff:** Much lower capital efficiency.

## Fee Tier Selection

Uniswap v3 offers four fee tiers:

| Fee | Typical Use |
|-----|-------------|
| 0.01% | Stablecoin pairs |
| 0.05% | Correlated assets |
| 0.30% | Standard pairs |
| 1.00% | Exotic pairs |

### Tier Strategy

Lower tiers attract more volume but earn less per trade. Higher tiers earn more per trade but see less activity.

**Analysis approach:**
1. Check current volume distribution across tiers
2. Calculate fee earnings: volume * fee rate * your share
3. Consider competition: overcrowded ranges earn less per LP

For most ETH/USDC positions, 0.05% has become dominant due to high volume concentration.

## Rebalancing Approaches

### Passive (Hold and Hope)

Set range and wait. If price leaves range, either:
- Wait for return
- Accept 100% exposure to one asset

**Pros:** Lowest gas costs, least management
**Cons:** Misses fees when out of range, crystallizes IL

### Active Rebalancing

When price approaches range edge:
1. Close position
2. Swap to rebalance token ratio
3. Open new position centered on current price

**Pros:** Maximizes time in range earning fees
**Cons:** Gas costs, realized IL, execution complexity

### Automated Management

Protocols like Arrakis, Gamma, and Bunni automate rebalancing:
- Monitor positions continuously
- Rebalance according to strategies
- Compound fees automatically

**Tradeoff:** Management fees (typically 1-10% of earned fees).

## Managing Impermanent Loss

Concentrated liquidity amplifies IL. A 2x price move in v2 causes 5.7% IL. The same move in a 10% range causes approximately 25% IL.

### IL Mitigation Strategies

**Wider ranges:** Accept lower capital efficiency for reduced IL risk.

**Hedging:** Use perpetual shorts or options to offset directional exposure.

**Correlated pairs:** ETH/stETH has minimal relative price movement, reducing IL significantly.

**Higher fee tiers:** More fees help offset IL, but volume must be sufficient.

### When IL is Acceptable

IL is the cost of earning fees. If your range generates 50% APY in fees but experiences 20% IL, net return is still strongly positive.

Calculate expected IL for your range using tools like Revert Finance before depositing.

## Position Sizing

### Concentration Risk

Putting all capital in one narrow range is extremely risky. Price leaving the range means zero fee income.

### Diversification Approaches

**Multiple ranges:** Split capital across several ranges around current price.

**Multiple pairs:** Diversify across different token pairs.

**Multiple chains:** Same strategy on Ethereum, Arbitrum, and Polygon.

### Example Portfolio

$100,000 total LP capital:
- $30,000: ETH/USDC 0.05%, tight range (-5%/+5%)
- $30,000: ETH/USDC 0.05%, medium range (-15%/+15%)
- $20,000: WBTC/ETH 0.30%, medium range
- $20,000: stETH/ETH 0.05%, very tight range

This balances fee potential with range coverage.

## Entry and Exit Timing

### Entering Positions

**During low volatility:** Less likely to immediately go out of range.

**After major price moves:** Volatility often decreases, good time to establish new ranges.

**Avoid:** Right before expected catalysts (earnings, upgrades, macro events).

### Exiting Positions

**When range goes stale:** If price consistently stays at one edge, fees suffer.

**Before expected volatility:** Major events can cause rapid exits from ranges.

**When better opportunities exist:** Reallocate to higher-APR pools.

## Gas Optimization

Ethereum mainnet v3 operations are expensive:
- Open position: $50-150
- Close position: $30-80
- Collect fees: $15-40

### Reducing Costs

**Use L2s:** Arbitrum and Polygon have v3 with 90%+ gas savings.

**Batch operations:** Some interfaces allow combining collect + close.

**Timing:** Execute during low gas periods (weekends, off-hours).

**Hold longer:** Fewer rebalances mean fewer gas costs.

## Common Mistakes

**Too narrow ranges:** Maximizing efficiency also maximizes risk of exiting range.

**Ignoring gas:** Frequent rebalancing can consume all fee profits.

**Chasing APY:** High displayed APRs often come from unsustainable ranges or low volume.

**Not collecting fees:** Uncollected fees do not compound automatically in v3.

## Summary

Concentrated liquidity offers dramatically improved capital efficiency for active LPs willing to manage positions. Success requires understanding range selection, rebalancing triggers, and IL dynamics.

Start with wider ranges to learn the mechanics. Gradually narrow as you understand your specific pair's behavior. Use analytics tools to track performance and adjust strategy.
