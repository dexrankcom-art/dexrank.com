## What is an Automated Market Maker?

An **automated market maker (AMM)** is a smart contract that provides liquidity for trades without traditional buyers and sellers. Instead of matching orders, AMMs use mathematical formulas to determine prices based on the ratio of tokens in a pool.

Before AMMs, decentralized trading required order books where users had to find counterparties. AMMs solved this by letting anyone trade instantly against a pool of tokens. This innovation made DEXs practical and sparked the DeFi explosion of 2020.

Today, AMMs process billions in daily volume across hundreds of protocols. Understanding how they work is essential for anyone using or building in DeFi.

## How Traditional Exchanges Work

On a traditional exchange (like the NYSE or Coinbase), trading uses an **order book**:

1. Buyers post bids: "I will buy 1 ETH at $2,000"
2. Sellers post asks: "I will sell 1 ETH at $2,010"
3. When a bid meets an ask, a trade executes
4. Market makers provide liquidity by posting both bids and asks

This requires:
- Active participants on both sides
- Sufficient order depth
- Fast matching engines
- Market makers with capital and algorithms

On-chain order books are expensive because every order submission, cancellation, and update costs gas. They work poorly with blockchain latency and throughput limits.

## How AMMs Solve This

AMMs replace order books with **liquidity pools** governed by mathematical formulas:

1. Liquidity providers deposit token pairs into pools
2. A formula (like x * y = k) determines the exchange rate
3. Traders swap against the pool at the formula-determined price
4. The price adjusts automatically based on supply and demand
5. No order matching, no waiting for counterparties

This is simpler, always available, and works without professional market makers. Anyone can provide liquidity; anyone can trade.

## The Constant Product Formula

Most AMMs use the **constant product** formula, invented by Uniswap:

**x * y = k**

Where:
- x = quantity of token A
- y = quantity of token B
- k = a constant that must remain unchanged after trades

### How Prices Are Determined

If a pool has 100 ETH and 200,000 USDC:
- k = 100 * 200,000 = 20,000,000
- Price of ETH = 200,000 / 100 = 2,000 USDC

When you buy 1 ETH:
- Pool must maintain k = 20,000,000
- New ETH: 99
- New USDC: 20,000,000 / 99 = 202,020
- You pay: 202,020 - 200,000 = 2,020 USDC
- Effective price: 2,020 USDC per ETH

The price rose from 2,000 to 2,040 (after the trade) because ETH became scarcer in the pool.

### The Bonding Curve

The constant product formula creates a **bonding curve**: a graphical representation of all possible pool states.

As you buy token A:
- A becomes scarcer, its price increases
- B becomes more abundant, its price decreases
- The curve ensures the pool never runs out of either token (prices go to infinity as either approaches zero)

This elegantly solves the liquidity problem. No matter how much you want to trade, the pool always has a price, though extremely large trades face extreme slippage.

## Price Impact and Slippage

**Price impact** measures how much your trade moves the market price. Larger trades relative to pool size create more impact.

**Slippage** is the difference between the expected price and the execution price.

Example with $1 million pool:
- $1,000 trade: 0.1% price impact
- $10,000 trade: 1% price impact
- $100,000 trade: 10% price impact

High TVL pools handle large trades with minimal slippage. Low TVL pools are only suitable for small trades.

## Liquidity Provider Mechanics

### Depositing

To add liquidity to an x * y = k pool:
1. Deposit equal dollar values of both tokens
2. Receive LP tokens representing your share
3. Example: depositing $5,000 ETH + $5,000 USDC into a $100,000 pool gives you 10% of LP tokens

### Earning Fees

Every trade pays a fee (typically 0.3%) that stays in the pool:
- Fee adds to token reserves
- LP token value increases proportionally
- Your share grows without additional deposits

If a $100,000 pool does $10,000 daily volume:
- Daily fees: $10,000 * 0.3% = $30
- Your 10% share earns: $3/day

### Withdrawing

When you remove liquidity:
1. Burn LP tokens
2. Receive your proportion of both tokens
3. The ratio may differ from when you deposited (impermanent loss)

## Different AMM Models

### Constant Product (Uniswap v1/v2)

The original model described above. Simple, works for any token pair, but capital efficiency is low because liquidity spreads across all possible prices.

**Pros**: Simple, battle-tested, works for volatile pairs
**Cons**: High slippage for large trades, poor capital efficiency

### Concentrated Liquidity (Uniswap v3)

LPs choose a price range to provide liquidity. Within that range, capital is used efficiently. Outside, the position earns nothing.

**Example**: Instead of providing liquidity from $0 to infinity, an LP provides only between $1,900-$2,100 for ETH/USDC.

**Pros**: 4000x capital efficiency in tight ranges
**Cons**: Requires active management, higher IL in narrow ranges

### Stable Pools (Curve)

Optimized formula for assets that should trade near 1:1 (stablecoins, wrapped tokens). The curve is flatter near the target price, reducing slippage dramatically.

**Pros**: Ultra-low slippage for stable swaps
**Cons**: Only works for pegged assets, poor for volatile pairs

### Weighted Pools (Balancer)

Pools can have custom ratios (80/20, 60/20/20) instead of just 50/50. Reduces exposure to one asset, changes IL dynamics.

**Pros**: Customizable exposure, multi-asset pools
**Cons**: More complex math, less intuitive

### Hybrid Models (Curve v2, Maverick)

Combine concentrated liquidity with dynamic parameters. The pool adjusts its curve based on recent trading patterns.

**Pros**: Adapts to market conditions automatically
**Cons**: Complex, newer and less tested

## AMM Innovations

### Virtual Reserves

Some AMMs use virtual reserves to improve capital efficiency. Tokens may not all be in the pool simultaneously; smart contracts calculate as if they were.

### Dynamic Fees

Instead of fixed 0.3%, some AMMs adjust fees based on volatility or pool utilization. Higher volatility means higher fees to protect LPs from arbitrage losses.

### Time-Weighted Average Price (TWAP)

AMMs can serve as price oracles by tracking time-weighted average prices. This resists manipulation better than spot prices.

### Hooks and Middleware

Uniswap v4 introduces hooks: custom code that runs before/after swaps. This enables programmable pool behavior like dynamic fees, limit orders, or MEV protection.

## Comparing AMM Types

| AMM Type | Best For | Capital Efficiency | IL Risk |
|----------|----------|-------------------|---------|
| Constant Product | General trading | Low | Moderate |
| Concentrated | Active LPs | High | High |
| Stable | Pegged assets | Very High | Very Low |
| Weighted | Index-like exposure | Medium | Varies |

## AMM Limitations

### Cannot Do Limit Orders Natively

AMMs execute at the current price, not a target price. Workarounds exist (range orders in v3, hook implementations) but are not as elegant as order books for precise execution.

### Impermanent Loss

The fundamental trade-off: providing liquidity means accepting rebalancing risk when prices diverge. See [Impermanent Loss Explained](/guides/impermanent-loss).

### MEV Vulnerability

Sandwich attacks extract value from AMM traders. A bot sees your pending trade, front-runs it to move the price, and back-runs to profit from your slippage. Private transactions and MEV protection help mitigate this.

### Oracle Manipulation

Relying on AMM prices for oracles can be dangerous. Low-liquidity pools can be manipulated by large trades. TWAP oracles and multiple price sources reduce this risk.

## The Future of AMMs

AMM design continues evolving:

- **Intent-based trading**: Users express what they want; solvers find optimal execution
- **Cross-chain AMMs**: Liquidity spanning multiple blockchains
- **Off-chain computation**: Calculating optimal routes off-chain, settling on-chain
- **Account abstraction**: Enabling gasless trades and smart wallet interactions

The core AMM concept remains: deterministic pricing through smart contracts. Implementations get more sophisticated, but the foundation is solid.

## Summary

AMMs replaced order books with mathematical formulas, enabling trustless, always-available trading on decentralized exchanges. The constant product formula (x * y = k) created the foundation, with innovations like concentrated liquidity and stable curves addressing specific use cases.

Understanding AMMs helps you:
- Choose the right DEX for your trades
- Evaluate LP opportunities
- Understand why slippage occurs
- Assess smart contract risks

AMMs are the engine powering DeFi trading. Learning how they work puts you ahead of most market participants.
